CYBERSECURITY RISK MANAGEMENT, STRATEGY, GOVERNANCE, AND INCIDENT DISCLOSURE

SECURITIES AND EXCHANGE COMMISSION
17 CFR Parts 229, 232, 239, 240, and 249
Release Nos. 33-11216; 34-97989; IC-35003; File No. S7-09-22

Adopted: July 26, 2023
Effective Date: September 5, 2023
Federal Register Publication: August 4, 2023 (88 FR 51896)

Source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/08/04/2023-15927/cybersecurity-risk-management-strategy-governance-and-incident-disclosure

AGENCY: Securities and Exchange Commission.

ACTION: Final rule.

SUMMARY: The Securities and Exchange Commission is adopting rules to enhance and standardize
disclosures regarding cybersecurity risk management, strategy, governance, and incidents by public
companies subject to the reporting requirements of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. The
Commission is adopting amendments to Form 8-K to require registrants to disclose material
cybersecurity incidents. The Commission is also adopting amendments to Regulation S-K to require
periodic disclosures about cybersecurity risk management, strategy, and governance.

================================================================================
ARTICLE I — DEFINITIONS
================================================================================

SECTION 1.1 CYBERSECURITY INCIDENT

The term "cybersecurity incident" means an unauthorized occurrence, or a series of related
unauthorized occurrences, on or conducted through a registrant's information systems that
jeopardizes the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of a registrant's information systems
or any information residing therein.

For purposes of this definition, a series of related unauthorized occurrences includes incidents
that are part of the same attack campaign, even if discovered or remediated at different times.
The determination of whether occurrences are related requires the registrant to assess whether
the incidents share a common threat actor, exploit a common vulnerability, or reflect a common
attack methodology.

SECTION 1.2 CYBERSECURITY THREAT

The term "cybersecurity threat" means any potential unauthorized occurrence on or conducted
through a registrant's information systems that may result in adverse effects on the
confidentiality, integrity, or availability of a registrant's information systems or any
information residing therein. Cybersecurity threats include, but are not limited to, ransomware
attacks, supply chain compromises, phishing campaigns, insider threats, and distributed
denial-of-service attacks.

SECTION 1.3 INFORMATION SYSTEMS

The term "information systems" means electronic information resources, owned or used by the
registrant, including physical or virtual infrastructure controlled by such information resources,
or components thereof, organized for the collection, processing, maintenance, use, sharing,
dissemination, or disposition of information. This term encompasses on-premises infrastructure,
cloud-based systems, third-party platforms used in the registrant's operations, and operational
technology systems connected to the registrant's networks.

SECTION 1.4 MATERIALITY STANDARD

The materiality determination for cybersecurity incidents shall apply the standard established
in TSC Industries, Inc. v. Northway, Inc., 426 U.S. 438 (1976) and Basic Inc. v. Levinson, 485
U.S. 224 (1988). An incident is material if there is a substantial likelihood that a reasonable
investor would consider it important in making an investment decision or if the information
would have significantly altered the total mix of available information. Registrants should
consider both quantitative and qualitative factors, including but not limited to: the scope and
nature of compromised data, operational disruption, regulatory exposure, litigation risk,
reputational harm, and financial impact.

ARTICLE II — MATERIAL CYBERSECURITY INCIDENT DISCLOSURE (FORM 8-K ITEM 1.05)
================================================================================

SECTION 2.1 DISCLOSURE OBLIGATION

A registrant shall disclose any cybersecurity incident that the registrant determines to be
material. The disclosure shall describe the material aspects of:

(a) the nature of the cybersecurity incident;
(b) the scope of the cybersecurity incident;
(c) the timing of the cybersecurity incident; and
(d) the material impact or reasonably likely material impact on the registrant, including on
its financial condition and results of operations.

SECTION 2.2 TIMING OF DISCLOSURE

Such disclosure shall be made within four business days after the registrant determines that
a cybersecurity incident it has experienced is material. The four-business-day period begins
upon the registrant's determination of materiality, not upon discovery of the incident.

Registrants should have processes and controls in place to make materiality determinations
promptly. The Commission does not expect registrants to engage in lengthy processes before
determining materiality; rather, registrants should make prompt assessments based on available
information and disclose once a materiality determination has been made.

SECTION 2.3 DELAYED DISCLOSURE — LAW ENFORCEMENT EXCEPTION

The United States Attorney General may notify the Commission that disclosure of a cybersecurity
incident would pose a substantial risk to national security or public safety. Upon receipt of
such notification, the Commission will delay the disclosure requirement. The delay may be
extended for additional 30-day periods upon renewed notification. Registrants invoking this
exception must have notified the Attorney General and received acknowledgment prior to the
deadline under Section 2.2.

SECTION 2.4 SUBSEQUENT DISCLOSURES

If information required by Item 1.05 is not available at the time of the initial disclosure,
the registrant must amend the previously filed Form 8-K within four business days after
determining, or reasonably should have determined, that additional material information is
available. Subsequent disclosures should update the description of the incident, its scope,
timing, and material impacts as that information becomes known.

SECTION 2.5 AGGREGATE DISCLOSURE OF IMMATERIAL INCIDENTS

Registrants are not required to disclose cybersecurity incidents that are not material.
However, if previously undisclosed immaterial cybersecurity incidents become material in the
aggregate, registrants should consider whether disclosure is warranted. The Commission expects
registrants to have processes for tracking and evaluating patterns of incidents.

ARTICLE III — ANNUAL DISCLOSURE REQUIREMENTS (REGULATION S-K § 229.106)
================================================================================

SECTION 3.1 CYBERSECURITY RISK MANAGEMENT (ITEM 106(a))

Describe the registrant's processes, if any, for assessing, identifying, and managing material
risks from cybersecurity threats in sufficient detail for a reasonable investor to understand
those processes. This disclosure shall address:

(1) whether and how the registrant has integrated cybersecurity risk assessments into its
overall risk management systems or processes;

(2) whether the registrant engages assessors, consultants, auditors, or other third parties
in connection with such processes; and

(3) whether the registrant has processes to oversee and identify material risks from
cybersecurity threats associated with its use of third-party service providers.

SECTION 3.2 MANAGEMENT'S ROLE (ITEM 106(b))

Describe management's role in assessing and managing the registrant's material risks from
cybersecurity threats. This disclosure shall describe:

(1) whether and which management positions or committees are responsible for assessing and
managing cybersecurity risks and the relevant expertise of such persons or members in such
detail as necessary to fully describe the nature of the expertise;

(2) the processes by which such persons or committees are informed about and monitor the
prevention, detection, mitigation, and remediation of cybersecurity incidents; and

(3) whether such persons or committees report information about cybersecurity risks and
incidents to the board of directors or a committee or subcommittee of the board of directors.

SECTION 3.3 BOARD OVERSIGHT (ITEM 106(c))

Describe the board of directors' oversight of risks from cybersecurity threats. If any board
committee or subcommittee is responsible for the oversight of cybersecurity risks, identify
any such committee or subcommittee and describe the processes by which the board or such
committee or subcommittee is informed about such risks.

A registrant need not disclose the name of the specific director(s) responsible for
cybersecurity oversight, but should describe the nature and frequency of reporting to the board.

ARTICLE IV — COMPLIANCE DATES AND TRANSITION PROVISIONS
================================================================================

SECTION 4.1 FORM 8-K COMPLIANCE DATES

Large accelerated filers (as defined in § 240.12b-2) must comply with the requirements of
Item 1.05 of Form 8-K beginning December 18, 2023.

All other registrants, except smaller reporting companies, must comply beginning June 15, 2024.

Smaller reporting companies (as defined in § 229.10(f)(1)) must comply beginning June 15, 2024.

SECTION 4.2 ANNUAL REPORT COMPLIANCE DATES

Large accelerated filers must comply with the requirements of Item 106 of Regulation S-K and
Item 1C of Form 10-K in annual reports for fiscal years ending on or after December 15, 2023.

All other registrants must comply in annual reports for fiscal years ending on or after
December 15, 2024.

SECTION 4.3 FOREIGN PRIVATE ISSUER COMPLIANCE

Foreign private issuers must disclose material cybersecurity incidents on Form 6-K promptly
after the registrant becomes aware of the incident and makes a determination of materiality.
Annual cybersecurity disclosures shall be made in the annual report on Form 20-F beginning
with fiscal years ending on or after December 15, 2023 for large accelerated filers and
December 15, 2024 for all other foreign private issuers.

SECTION 4.4 STRUCTURED DATA REQUIREMENTS

Registrants must tag cybersecurity disclosures made pursuant to Item 1.05 of Form 8-K and
Item 106 of Regulation S-K using Inline XBRL (iXBRL) in accordance with the EDGAR XBRL
Technical Specifications. The iXBRL tagging requirement applies:

(a) for Form 8-K Item 1.05: beginning December 18, 2024 for large accelerated filers and
June 15, 2025 for all other registrants; and

(b) for Regulation S-K Item 106: one year after the registrant's first annual report filing
that includes Item 106 disclosures.

ARTICLE V — CROSS-REFERENCES AND RELATED REQUIREMENTS
================================================================================

SECTION 5.1 RELATIONSHIP TO EXISTING DISCLOSURE OBLIGATIONS

The requirements of this rule supplement, and do not replace, existing disclosure obligations
under the Securities Act and the Exchange Act, including disclosure obligations arising from
Items 1A, 7, and 9A of Form 10-K and the general anti-fraud provisions of the federal
securities laws. Registrants should continue to evaluate cybersecurity-related information
under existing materiality standards applicable to all disclosures.

See also: Item 1A (Risk Factors) — material cybersecurity risks should be described.
See also: Item 7 (MD&A) — material impacts of cybersecurity incidents on financial condition
and results of operations must be discussed.
See also: Form 8-K Item 1.05 (this rule).
See also: Form 20-F Item 16K (foreign private issuers).

SECTION 5.2 RELATIONSHIP TO STATE AND INTERNATIONAL REQUIREMENTS

Nothing in this rule limits or supersedes state cybersecurity notification laws, including
requirements under state data breach notification statutes. Registrants operating under state
laws with shorter notification windows (e.g., New York 72-hour requirement for certain
financial institutions) must comply with the more stringent applicable requirement.

Registrants subject to European Union regulations, including the Network and Information
Security Directive (NIS2) and the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA), should
coordinate disclosures under this rule with their obligations under applicable EU law.

EXHIBIT A — SUMMARY OF COMPLIANCE OBLIGATIONS
================================================================================

SECTION A.1 COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST

The following is a non-exhaustive summary of actions registrants should consider:

1. INCIDENT RESPONSE PROTOCOL
   - Establish or update incident response plan to include materiality determination process
   - Define escalation path from IT/security team to General Counsel and CEO within 24 hours
     of suspected material incident
   - Document decision-making framework for materiality assessments

2. GOVERNANCE DOCUMENTATION
   - Assign board committee responsibility for cybersecurity oversight (or document that the
     full board retains this function)
   - Update board committee charter(s) to reflect cybersecurity oversight role
   - Establish regular board briefing cadence on cybersecurity risks and incidents

3. ANNUAL REPORT PREPARATION
   - Draft Item 1C cybersecurity section template for Form 10-K
   - Identify management positions responsible for cybersecurity risk management
   - Document third-party service provider oversight process
   - Identify certifying officers and update sub-certification processes

4. DISCLOSURE CONTROLS
   - Add cybersecurity incident trigger to disclosure controls and procedures checklist
   - Brief Disclosure Committee on new Form 8-K Item 1.05 requirements
   - Establish process for monitoring incidents across subsidiary and affiliated entities

5. XBRL TAGGING
   - Identify XBRL taxonomy elements for cybersecurity disclosures
   - Update EDGAR filing systems to support iXBRL tagging by applicable compliance dates
